The Materials That Should Exist

Biomaterials are shaped by interacting chemical, structural and biological factors that are difficult to optimise one at a time. We use experimental data and computational models to map these relationships, predict outcomes and identify candidate formulations for experimental testing.

Research approach

Designing biomaterials across interacting variables.

A biomaterial is not defined by composition alone. Its chemistry, structure and biological environment interact over time, and changes in one feature can affect several outcomes.

Our workflow treats these relationships as a connected design problem. Experimental material and biological data are used to identify influential variables, build predictive models and search for formulations that are more likely to meet predefined targets.

Each round of experiments provides new information about the design space and helps determine which measurements, formulations or conditions should be examined next.

Design workflow

A data-guided workflow for biomaterials design.

The workflow moves through four linked stages. MAP identifies influential variables in the design space. PREDICT uses measured data to estimate later or unmeasured outcomes. PRESCRIBE searches for formulations that match predefined targets, and VALIDATE compares those predictions with new experimental results.

Optimisation

Optimising formulations under competing property targets.

Material properties do not always improve in the same direction. Increasing radiopacifier content can improve radiopacity while reducing strength, while increasing water can improve flow but delay setting. The genetic algorithm therefore evaluates candidate formulations against several selected property targets at the same time.

Each candidate formulation is scored against the target profile. Better-matching candidates contribute to subsequent generations, shifting the population toward regions of the design space that better satisfy the selected constraints. The result is a search for the best overall compromise rather than a formulation that maximises every property independently.

Workflow extension

Extending the design workflow toward translation.

MAP, PREDICT, PRESCRIBE and VALIDATE describe the current research workflow. New experimental results can be returned to the dataset to refine subsequent predictions and design decisions. TRANSLATE represents the longer-term extension of this process, in which validated materials and models are evaluated under increasingly clinically relevant conditions.

Digital BioScore

Integrating multiple dimensions of cell response.

A material can perform differently across proliferation, migration and mineralisation assays, so no single endpoint provides a complete description of its in vitro biological response. The Digital BioScore combines these measurements into an integrated profile while preserving the contribution of each individual domain. The holographic representation is used to visualise how these domains vary together across materials.

Five-year horizon

Future directions: patient-specific prediction and automated discovery.

The next stage of the programme is to connect material design with patient-level information and increasingly automated experimentation. The longer-term objective is to move from formulations developed for average conditions toward candidates selected according to the biological and clinical constraints of individual cases.

CLINICAL PREDICTION

Models that combine material characteristics with patient-level data to estimate how candidate materials may perform under the conditions of a specific case.

AUTONOMOUS DISCOVERY

Experimental systems that can propose, test and refine candidate formulations iteratively, while scientists define the biological question, objectives and constraints.

HOW THE STUDIES CONNECT Together, these studies establish components of a workflow in which experimental design narrows the space to be explored, predictive models estimate outcomes that have not yet been measured, and optimisation methods identify candidate formulations for targeted testing. Each stage remains anchored to experimental data and is evaluated against measured outcomes.
Research principle

The role of AI in the experimental workflow.

Across the programme, AI and statistical modelling are used to organise complex design spaces, identify informative variables, estimate outcomes and search for candidate formulations. The scientific questions, experimental constraints and interpretation remain defined by researchers, and computational predictions are evaluated against laboratory evidence.
