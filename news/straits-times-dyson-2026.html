The Straits Times highlights A/P Rosa’s research collaboration with Dyson

A five-year collaboration between NUS Dentistry and Dyson demonstrates how academic research in oral biofilms can contribute to industrial innovation and technology development.

The Straits Times has featured a research collaboration between Associate Professor Vinicius Rosa of the National University of Singapore (NUS) Faculty of Dentistry and Dyson, highlighting the role of academic expertise in supporting the development of new technologies.

Published on 10 September 2026, the article explores Singapore’s contributions to Dyson’s new CameraJet smart toothbrush, including a five-year research collaboration with NUS Dentistry to develop proxy plaque — a synthetic material that reproduces aspects of dental plaque adhesion and enables controlled laboratory testing.

A/P Rosa, who leads research in novel diagnostics and therapeutics at NUS Dentistry, explained how his team’s investigations into oral biofilms and their interactions with dental enamel contributed to the scientific understanding underpinning the collaboration. This research helped inform the development of experimental models that Dyson could use to evaluate and refine its engineering technologies.

The partnership brought together complementary expertise in oral biology, dental materials and engineering. While the NUS Dentistry team was not directly involved in developing the CameraJet toothbrush, its scientific contribution supported the broader research and testing process.

Speaking to The Straits Times, Dyson founder James Dyson also praised the collaboration, describing NUS Dentistry as “brilliant” and recognising the value of having such academic expertise in Singapore.

Beyond the specific technology, the collaboration illustrates how fundamental research in dentistry can extend beyond traditional academic settings, providing scientific knowledge and experimental approaches that help industry address complex technical challenges.
