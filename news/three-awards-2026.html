Three awards recognise a shared vision for the dental materials that should exist

Three distinct research projects bring together materials science, biology and artificial intelligence to advance how dental biomaterials are designed, developed and evaluated.

In 2026, Associate Professor Vinicius Rosa and PhD candidate Clarice Sabino received three research awards from the International Association for Dental, Oral and Craniofacial Research (IADR) and the Academy of Dental Materials (ADM). Rosa received the IADR Innovation in Oral Care Award, while Sabino received the IADR Southeast Asian Division Hatton Award (Senior Category) and the ADM George C. Paffenbarger Award.

The three projects address complementary scientific challenges: developing more biologically representative testing environments, integrating cellular responses into quantitative digital representations, and using computational models to guide biomaterials development.

Rethinking how dental materials are developed and evaluated

Developing dental biomaterials requires understanding how their physical and chemical properties relate to biological performance. These dimensions are frequently investigated through separate experimental approaches.

Conventional in vitro assays, including extract-based testing and two-dimensional cell cultures, provide valuable information but capture selected aspects of material–cell interactions. Other biological outcomes, such as cellular differentiation and mineralisation, may require several weeks of experimental observation.

These challenges motivate approaches that connect material properties with biological responses and use the resulting information to guide formulation development. The three award-winning projects explore different aspects of this research direction.

IADR Innovation in Oral Care Award: creating more human-relevant testing environments

A/P Rosa was one of three recipients worldwide of the 2026 IADR Innovation in Oral Care Award, presented by IADR and sponsored by Haleon. Announced in March, the award was presented during the 104th IADR General Session in San Diego.

His project, AI-Enabled Biomimetic Hydrogels for Biosafety Testing of Dental Materials, investigates the integration of artificial intelligence and biomimetic hydrogels to develop more biologically informative platforms for evaluating dental materials.

Hydrogels offer three-dimensional cellular environments with adjustable mechanical and biochemical properties that reproduce selected features of the extracellular matrix. Machine learning can support the exploration of relationships between hydrogel composition, material properties and cellular responses, helping guide experimental design and interpretation.

The project is part of Rosa’s broader AI Living Lab research programme, featured in The Straits Times in May 2026 for its integration of artificial intelligence and tissue-like experimental models in oral healthcare research.

IADR Hatton Award: making cellular responses measurable and comparable

In September, Clarice Sabino received the IADR Southeast Asian Division Hatton Award in the Senior Category at the division’s annual meeting in Phnom Penh, Cambodia.

Her project, Digital BioScore: Integrating Materials and Cells for AI-driven Biomaterials Design, investigates how responses of dental pulp stem cells to different dental cements can be integrated into quantitative, multidimensional digital representations.

By combining measurements of cellular survival, proliferation, differentiation and mineralisation, the approach seeks to characterise biological performance through the collective response of cells rather than isolated experimental outcomes. These integrated profiles may support comparisons between material formulations and provide biological information for future computational modelling.

The Hatton competition is one of IADR’s established programmes supporting early-career researchers. Divisional winners are selected to represent their regions at the international competition, where Sabino will represent the Southeast Asian Division in 2027.

ADM Paffenbarger Award: predicting the biological performance of calcium silicate cements

Just three weeks later, on 9 October, Sabino received the George C. Paffenbarger Award at the Academy of Dental Materials annual meeting in Tokyo.

Her project, Novel AI-Driven Hyperdimensional Regenerative Atlas for Screening CSCs Biological Performance (HYDRA), proposes a computational framework to investigate and predict the biological performance of calcium silicate cements.

These materials are widely used in procedures involving living dental tissues. Their biological effects are influenced by interacting physicochemical characteristics, including ion release and changes in local pH, which can affect cellular behaviour and mineralisation.

HYDRA aims to integrate evidence mapping, predictive modelling and experimental validation into a Hyperdimensional Regenerative Atlas, supporting the identification and prioritisation of promising material formulations for biological investigation.

The team’s previously published research in Dental Materials demonstrates the feasibility of using material informatics to predict long-term physicochemical behaviour. Using pH measurements collected at three and 24 hours, together with specimen surface area, a machine-learning model predicted the pH evolution of calcium silicate cements over 28 days. Experimental validation across commercial and experimental formulations demonstrated strong agreement between predicted and measured values.

Although this study addressed a physicochemical property rather than biological outcomes, it provides a methodological foundation for extending predictive modelling towards the biological questions addressed by HYDRA.

A recognition spanning two generations

The Paffenbarger Award holds particular significance for Rosa and Sabino.

In 2007, while a student at the University of São Paulo, Rosa received first place in the same award at the ADM meeting in Fort Lauderdale, Florida, for research investigating ion exchange and the mechanical behaviour of dental porcelain.

Nineteen years later, his PhD student received the award for research exploring artificial intelligence as a tool for understanding and predicting the biological performance of dental materials.

The two projects address different scientific questions, reflecting the evolution of research methods and opportunities in dental materials science. Their recognition through the same award also connects two stages of an academic career: from conducting experimental research on dental ceramics to supervising work that integrates materials science, cell biology and computational modelling.

A collaborative research direction

Sabino’s Digital BioScore and HYDRA projects were developed under Rosa’s supervision at the NUS Faculty of Dentistry, in collaboration with Professor Nick Silikas from the University of Manchester and Anastasiia Grymak from the University of Otago.

Although independently conceived, the three recognised projects contribute to a broader research direction that connects material composition, physicochemical properties and biological responses through experimental and computational approaches.

The longer-term objective is to move beyond evaluating existing formulations towards identifying and developing dental biomaterials with defined properties and desired biological performance.
