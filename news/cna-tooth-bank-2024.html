CNA interviews A/P Rosa about Singapore's first Tooth Tissue Bank

The Asia First interview examined how a shared bank of donated extracted teeth can make dental research faster and more reproducible.

Channel NewsAsia interviewed A/P Vinicius Rosa on Asia First about Singapore's first Tooth Tissue Bank and its role in supporting research at NUS Dentistry and the National University Centre for Oral Health Singapore (NUCOHS).

Launched in 2021, the bank gives researchers access to donated extracted teeth that can be prepared for defined experimental needs. The interview discussed how this infrastructure reduces the time required to source specimens and supports work ranging from dental-material testing to regenerative research involving dental pulp cells and tissues.

Every extracted tooth is a potential contribution to the future of regenerative dentistry.

Public science communication is an increasingly important dimension of research leadership in Singapore. A/P Rosa’s engagement with mainstream media — from broadcast television to print and digital outlets — reflects NUS Dentistry’s commitment to translating scientific work into public understanding. The CNA segment reached an audience extending well beyond the academic research community, helping to raise awareness of how dental research connects to Singapore’s broader ambitions in biomedical science and healthcare innovation.

About the Tooth Tissue Bank

The NUCOHS Tooth Tissue Bank at the National University Centre for Oral Health Singapore systematically collects dental pulp and periodontal ligament tissue from extracted teeth donated by consenting patients. The tissue is cryopreserved and made available to researchers studying dental stem cells, tissue engineering, and regenerative endodontics.
